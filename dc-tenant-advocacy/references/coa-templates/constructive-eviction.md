# Constructive eviction (D.C.)

**Authority:** common law; D.C. formulation `[model knowledge — verify against DCCA case law via CourtListener before relying]`.

## Elements (tenant asserting)

| # | Element | Typical evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Landlord's act or omission (including failure to repair) | Repair requests, inspection reports | `[model knowledge — verify]` |
| 2 | That substantially interfered with the tenant's beneficial use and enjoyment of the premises | Photos, testimony, inspector findings, medical records if health-related | `[model knowledge — verify]` |
| 3 | Landlord had notice and a reasonable opportunity to cure | Dated notices | `[model knowledge — verify]` |
| 4 | Tenant vacated (or vacated the affected part) within a reasonable time because of the interference | Move-out date, relocation costs | `[model knowledge — verify]` — vacating is traditionally required; confirm whether D.C. recognizes partial constructive eviction |
| 5 | Damages | Relocation costs, rent paid during interference, difference in rent → `damages-calculator` | `[model knowledge — verify]` |

## Notes

- If the tenant has not vacated, habitability (`warranty-of-habitability.md`) is usually the better vehicle.
- Strongest when paired with retaliation evidence showing the interference was deliberate.
